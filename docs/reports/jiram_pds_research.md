## 1. Archive location and layout

- **PDS node**: PDS Atmospheres Node (NMSU), hosted at both `pds-atmospheres.nmsu.edu` and mirror `atmos.nmsu.edu`. Source 1: page titled "JUNO JIRAM" is the node's JIRAM landing page.
- **Format duality — PDS3 and PDS4 coexist**. Source 1, quoted: "Cruise and Earth Flyby Data and Jupiter Data with both PDS3 and PDS4 labels are available through perijove 65" (a later crawl of source 2 said "through perijove 71" — the page is a live, continuously updated listing, so the perijove cutoff is a moving target, not a fixed number).
- **PDS3 side**: classic volume-based archive, volumes named `JNOJIR_xxxx` (e.g. `JNOJIR_2033`), mirrored at the PDS Ring-Moon Systems/SETI "holdings" site: `pds-rings.seti.org/holdings/volumes/JNOJIR_xxxx/JNOJIR_2033/DATA/JIR_IMG_RDR_2021106T003758_V01.LBL` (source 4 — URL/naming pattern seen in a WebSearch result; the page itself 403'd on direct fetch, so treat the exact naming as UNVERIFIED-by-fetch but plausible/consistent with everything else found).
- **PDS4 side**: a single "bundle" `juno_jiram_bundle` (root file `bundle_juno_jiram.xml`) at `pds-atmospheres.nmsu.edu/PDS/data/PDS4/juno_jiram_bundle/` (source 18). Top-level subdirectories: `calibration/`, `context/`, `data_calibrated/`, `data_raw/`, `document/`, `xml_schema/`.
- **PDS4 bundle internal structure**: `data_calibrated/` is organized **by orbit**, one subdirectory per perijove: `orbit01/ ... orbit77/`, with gaps at orbit59, orbit60, orbit69, orbit71, orbit74 (source 19 — these gaps match the "no data / instrument issues" perijoves called out in source 20). The `data_calibrated/` directory also carries `collection_data_calibrated.xml` and a 26 MB `collection_data_calibrated_inventory.csv` — this collection inventory CSV is effectively the master per-file manifest for the calibrated collection and is the natural machine-readable index for a cataloging tool.
- **EDR vs RDR**: EDR = raw/uncalibrated instrument telemetry (source 1 calls these "Experiment Data Records" in the SIS; SIS source 3 labels example: `PRODUCT_TYPE = EDR`, `PROCESSING_LEVEL_ID = "2"`, `DATA_SET_ID = "JNO-J-JIRAM-2-EDR-V1.0"`). RDR = calibrated, radiometrically converted product (SIS example: `PRODUCT_TYPE = RDR`, `PROCESSING_LEVEL_ID = "3"`, `DATA_SET_ID = "JNO-J-JIRAM-3-RDR-V1.0"`, `SAMPLE_TYPE = IEEE_REAL`, 32-bit floats in physical radiance units).
- **Imager vs spectrometer**: JIRAM "combines 2 data channels in one instrument: the imager and the spectrometer, which are housed in the same optical subsystem" (source 6, from the .ti instrument-kernel comments as summarized by WebFetch). Instrument IDs from the IK (source 6): `JUNO_JIRAM_I` (-61410, full imager), `JUNO_JIRAM_I_LBAND` (-61411), `JUNO_JIRAM_I_MBAND` (-61412), `JUNO_JIRAM_S` (-61420, spectrometer).
- **Imager filters**: source 1, quoted: "The imager is equipped with L-band (3.45 μm with a 290-nm bandwidth) (for aurora observations) and M-band (4.78 μm with a 480-nm bandwidth) (for observations of thermal emissions) filters." SIS (source 3) confirms band edges in a label comment: "Bandpass L = 3320-3600 nm and M = 4537-5035 nm."
- **PDS3 archive-volume directory layout** (from SIS §6, source 3), same skeleton for EDR and RDR volumes: `ROOT/AAREADME.TXT`, `ERRATA.TXT`, `[DOCUMENT]` (SIS PDF+LBL), `[CALIB]` (calibration matrices — empty for EDR), `[DATA]` (science + auxiliary files with detached labels: `JIR_SPE_EDR/RDR_*.DAT/.LBL`, `JIR_IMG_EDR/RDR_*.IMG/.LBL`, `JIR_LOG_*_*.TAB/.LBL`), `[INDEX]` (`JIR_STATUS_*.CSV/.LBL`, `INDXINFO.TXT`, `INDEX.TAB`, `INDEX.LBL`), `[CATALOG]` (dataset/mission/instrument/person .CAT files), `[LABEL]` (`.FMT` structure-definition files), `VOLDESC.CAT`.
- Labels are **PDS3, detached** (`.LBL` files pointing at separate `.IMG`/`.DAT`/`.TAB` files), 80-column ASCII, CR-LF line endings (SIS §7.3, source 3), in addition to the newer PDS4 XML-labeled bundle.

## 2. Scale

- **Perijoves covered**: data exist from perijove 1 (Aug 2016) through at least perijove 77 as of the most recent crawl of the PDS4 `data_calibrated/` tree (source 19), with known gaps: PJ56 ("No image or spectral files were archived"), PJ59 ("No JIRAM data were obtained in PJ59 due to issues with the instrument"), PJ60 (data unsuitable for calibration), PJ61–62 (no archived image/spectral files, problem-investigation only), and PJ69–71 (electrical issues) (sources 2, 20). **This is a moving target**: Juno's prime mission nominally ended at PJ76 (Sept 2025) with a subsequent multi-year extended mission (per WebSearch news snippets — source 22, UNVERIFIED by direct fetch), and the archive's own front pages disagree with each other on the exact "available through perijove N" cutoff (65 vs 71 in two different fetches of sibling pages), confirming it is continuously updated. **I could not establish a single authoritative "as of today" perijove count.**
- **Number of image products**: not established as a precise total. Point data found: "During the 12th Juno perijove passage, JIRAM acquired more than 210 individual images arranged in 16 sequences" (WebSearch synthesis, UNVERIFIED by direct fetch) and "Thousands of images have been obtained by JIRAM" through perijove 6 alone (source 10, Planetary Society, paraphrased from page content). With ~70+ perijoves now archived, a total in the tens-of-thousands of image products (summed across L-band, M-band and spectral cubes) is plausible but **not directly confirmed by any single authoritative count I could fetch**.
- **Total archive size**: not established with confidence. A WebSearch summary asserted "The compressed file of JIRAM calibrated data (RDR data files) is 18 Gb," attributed to an NMSU JIRAM access page, but I could not reproduce or verify this quote on any page I actually fetched (source 17, the closest matching page, does not contain it) — **mark UNVERIFIED**. The PDS4 `data_calibrated/` collection inventory CSV alone is 26 MB just for the manifest (source 19), implying a large number of listed files, but this is metadata size, not data volume. **No verified GB/TB total for the full EDR+RDR archive was found.**
- **Derived/mosaic products are much smaller and individually sized** (source 17, `jiram_cp.html`): a global cylindrical radiance map (PJ02–PJ03, 4.5–5 μm) is 93 MB (CDF); north/south polar H3+ auroral mosaics (PJ3–PJ4, 3.3–3.6 μm) are 132–150 MB each (4 files); a Ganymede map (PJ24) is 1.5 MB; Io depth maps (12 wavelengths) are 1.5 MB each.

## 3. File format details

- **Image dimensions**: confirmed **432 samples (cross-track) × 256 lines (along-track)** for the full imager (both L- and M-band each occupy the full 432×256 frame in the archived EDR/RDR `.IMG`/`.LBL` examples — SIS §10.10/10.2, source 3: `LINES = 256`, `LINE_SAMPLES = 432`). Also cross-confirmed independently: "JIRAM images measure only 432 pixels horizontally" (source 10, Planetary Society). The IK (source 6) separately gives narrower along-track FOVs for the L-band/M-band 128-line sub-windows (1.744° each) versus the full-imager 3.6242° FOV — i.e., L and M can each be windowed to 128 of the 256 lines, but the standard product frame is 432×256.
- **Data type**: EDR raw images are `SAMPLE_TYPE = LSB_INTEGER`, `SAMPLE_BITS = 16` (16-bit signed integers, little-endian) (SIS §10.2, source 3). RDR calibrated images are `SAMPLE_TYPE = IEEE_REAL`, `SAMPLE_BITS = 32` (32-bit floats) (SIS §10.10, source 3).
- **Calibrated radiance units**: RDR image label field `UNIT = "W/(m^2*sr)"` (band radiance); RDR spectral (SPE) label field `UNIT = "W/(m^2*sr*micron)"` (spectral radiance) (SIS §10.10/10.11, source 3).
- **Time keywords**: `START_TIME` / `STOP_TIME` in `YYYY-MM-DDThh:mm:ss.fff` UTC (e.g. `START_TIME = 2022-01-12T09:22:01.269`), and `SPACECRAFT_CLOCK_START_COUNT` / `..._STOP_COUNT` as quoted strings, e.g. `"5/695251710:43909"` (SIS §7.1.1 and label examples, source 3).
- **Filter/channel identification**: `CHANNEL_ID` ("0"=Spectrum IR, "1"=Image), `INSTRUMENT_MODE_ID` (e.g. `SCI_I1_S1`) with `INSTRUMENT_MODE_DESC` spelling out "IMAGE(256x432) full acquisition, SPECTRUM (256x336)..." (SIS label examples, source 3). Filter/band identity for images is implicit in the mode/product naming (`JIR_IMG_...`) rather than a single explicit "L or M" label keyword in the excerpts examined — SIS Table 25 defines a local keyword `JNO:TARGET_PRESENCE_FLAG` that encodes per-target presence separately for spectrum, filter-L image, and filter-M image.
- **Geometry — key finding, likely relevant to the cataloging tool**: the PDS3 label itself does **not** carry per-pixel or per-frame geometry (no `SUB_SPACECRAFT_LATITUDE/LONGITUDE`, no footprint corner coordinates, no `EMISSION_ANGLE`/`INCIDENCE_ANGLE` keywords were found anywhere in the 90-page SIS, source 3, despite an explicit search). The label's "Geometry Information" section (SIS Fig. 14) instead resolves to a single `SPICE_FILE_NAME` list enumerating the exact SPICE kernel files used ("Names of the SPICE kernels used in producing the geometry information in the data product" — SIS Table 24, source 3), e.g. `{"juno_v09.tf","juno_jiram_v02.ti","juno_struct_v01.ti","naif0012.tls","pck00010.tpc","JNO_SCLKSCET.00130.tsc","de440s.bsp","juno_struct_v04.bsp","jup363.bsp","juno_rec_orbit.orb","spk_rec_211222_220204_220210.bsp","juno_sc_rec_220111_220112_v01.bc"}`. The one geometry-adjacent scalar keyword actually defined is `SUB_SOLAR_LONGITUDE` (SIS Table 24). The `JIR_LOG_*.TAB` "auxiliary data" files (SIS §10.9, Table 28) instead carry per-frame **engineering/housekeeping** telemetry (temperatures, gains, exposure/delay times, nadir-offset mirror angle, compression status) — not footprint geometry either. **Conclusion: a cataloging tool cannot get footprint/emission/incidence geometry from the archived labels or logs directly — it must compute it itself from the listed SPICE kernels** (this is exactly what the `spacecraft_rs` tool below does).
- **Index tables**: SIS §6.2 (source 3) confirms each PDS3 archive volume's `[INDEX]` directory contains `INDEX.TAB` ("a table listing all Data Products on this Volume") with matching `INDEX.LBL`, plus `INDXINFO.TXT`; there is also a `CUMINDEX.TAB` cumulative index maintained online at the discipline node ("An online and web accessible cumulative index file is maintained at the PDS discipline node while archive volumes are being produced. Only the last archive volume in the volume series will contain a cumulative index file" — SIS §6.2). For the PDS4 side, the closest analogue found is the `collection_data_calibrated_inventory.csv` (26 MB) in the bundle (source 19) — a collection-level product inventory, not confirmed to carry time/geometry columns itself (likely just LIDVID + file path per PDS4 convention; not verified in detail).

## 4. Geometry and navigation (SPICE)

- Confirmed kernel categories exist for Juno at NAIF (source 5, `naif.jpl.nasa.gov/pub/naif/JUNO/kernels/`): `ck/` (orientation), `fk/` (frames), `ik/` (instrument), `lsk/` (leapseconds), `pck/` (planetary constants), `sclk/` (spacecraft clock correlation), `spk/` (ephemeris/trajectory).
- **JIRAM instrument kernel** confirmed present: `naif.jpl.nasa.gov/pub/naif/JUNO/kernels/ik/juno_jiram_v02.ti` (source 6), defining FOVs/IDs for the imager (full, L-band-only, M-band-only) and the spectrometer, focal length 159.82033 mm, f/3.7, IFOV 0.000237767 rad/pixel, 38 μm pixels.
- A concrete real-label example (SIS, source 3) lists the exact kernel set actually used to geolocate one JIRAM product: frame kernel `juno_v09.tf`, IK `juno_jiram_v02.ti`, spacecraft structure kernel `juno_struct_v01.ti`, leapseconds `naif0012.tls`, planetary constants `pck00010.tpc`, SCLK `JNO_SCLKSCET.00130.tsc`, planetary ephemeris `de440s.bsp`, spacecraft structure SPK `juno_struct_v04.bsp`, Jupiter-system satellite ephemeris `jup363.bsp`, and reconstructed trajectory/orientation kernels (`juno_rec_orbit.orb`, `spk_rec_211222_220204_220210.bsp`, `juno_sc_rec_220111_220112_v01.bc`) — i.e. a full standard SPICE meta-kernel set (SPK+CK+IK+FK+SCLK+LSK+PCK) is required per product, and the exact file names are recorded per-product in the label so a cataloging/geometry tool can reconstruct exactly which kernels to load.
- **Published map-projected/mosaicked products**: confirmed to exist as CDF-format "derived products" (source 17, `jiram_cp.html`): a PJ02–PJ03 global cylindrical radiance map; PJ3–PJ4 north/south polar auroral H3+ mosaics; a PJ24 Ganymede map; multi-wavelength Io depth maps. Separately, a WebSearch snippet (not independently fetched/verified) describes JIRAM south-pole auroral mosaics built with "geometric information derived through SPICE-based routines...corrected for emission angle and then reprojected in stereographic maps to a pixel resolution of approximately 55 km" — consistent with, but not confirming beyond, the CDF products above (mark this specific stereographic-projection detail UNVERIFIED by direct fetch).

## 5. Existing tools

- **pds4_tools** — https://github.com/Small-Bodies-Node/pds4_tools — Python package (PDS Small Bodies Node) for reading/visualizing generic PDS4 products; applicable to the PDS4 `juno_jiram_bundle` but not JIRAM-specific. (Info via WebSearch summary; not independently re-fetched — treat description as moderately reliable, not a direct quote.)
- **pdr (Planetary Data Reader)** — https://github.com/MillionConcepts/pdr — general-purpose "single function to read all PDS data into Python," explicitly supports both PDS3 and PDS4, and is funded by NASA PDART specifically to cover "over two petabytes of observational planetary science data stored in the Planetary Data System" (JOSS paper, source 12, quoted: "pdr reads data products held by the Planetary Data System (PDS) that follow either PDS3 ... or PDS4 ... standards"). Not JIRAM-specific but designed to auto-handle attached/detached PDS3 labels like JIRAM's.
- **spacecraft_rs** — https://github.com/henrikmelin/spacecraft_rs — "Spacecraft Remote Sensing" (source 14). Directly relevant: computes per-pixel viewing/footprint geometry for spacecraft camera/spectrometer data using NAIF SPICE kernels, with a worked JIRAM example (`juno_jiram_example.py`, `juno_jiram_geometry()`); quoted: "calculates the viewing geometry for cameras and spectrometers mounted on spacecraft orbiting planets in our solar system" and "The software will not function without the spacecraft pointing information provided by ... NAIF kernels." This is exactly the gap identified in §3 above (PDS labels lack footprint/angle geometry).
- **juno-jiram-preview** — https://github.com/henrikmelin/juno-jiram-preview — same author; a small repo of JIRAM data-product notes/preview files, useful mainly as documentation of the four `JUNO_JIRAM_*` product/frame names (S, I, I_LBAND, I_MBAND).
- **JIRAM_paper_code** — https://github.com/liasiegelman/JIRAM_paper_code — repo owned by Lia Siegelman, a co-author of the Nature Astronomy 2022 vorticity/divergence paper (§6 below); contains `frontogenesis.py` and `moist_convection.py` but has no README/description, so its exact relationship to the published paper's velocity-field pipeline could not be confirmed from the page alone (plausible companion code, not confirmed).
- No PDS-Atmospheres-node-hosted "official JIRAM tool list" page was located in this search; the SIS (source 3) and the archive's own directory browsers (sources 18–20) are the closest things to an official cataloging aid, plus the PDS4 `collection_data_calibrated_inventory.csv` manifest.

## 6. The Nature Astronomy paper (s41550-022-01774)

- **Title**: "Vorticity and divergence at scales down to 200 km within and around the polar cyclones of Jupiter"
- **Authors** (full list, from the actual paper PDF, source 8): Andrew P. Ingersoll, Shawn P. Ewald, Federico Tosi, Alberto Adriani, Alessandro Mura, Davide Grassi, Christina Plainaki, Giuseppe Sindoni, Cheng Li, Lia Siegelman, Patrice Klein, William R. Young.
- **DOI**: 10.1038/s41550-022-01774-0 (confirmed independently by source 9, Semantic Scholar API JSON, and by source 8's own metadata header "Publisher's version (DOI) 10.1038/s41550-022-01774-0").
- **Journal**: Nature Astronomy, volume 6 (2022) (source 8 metadata: "Journal NATURE ASTRONOMY", "Volume 6"; corroborating WebSearch snippet gave pages 1280–1286, UNVERIFIED by direct fetch of that specific detail).
- **What data it released**: quoted directly from the paper's Data Availability section (source 8): "JIRAM data are available at the Planetary Data System (PDS) online (https://pds-atmospheres.nmsu.edu/data_and_services/atmospheres_data/JUNO/jiram). The filenames of the images are in Supplementary Table S1. Source data used in this report include calibrated, geometrically controlled, radiance data mapped onto an orthographic projection centered on the north pole and velocity vectors derived from the radiance data. These data products, about 150 MB in volume, are Source Data Tables SD1, SD2, and SD3." So: (a) the underlying raw JIRAM imagery is just the standard PDS archive (cited by filename in a supplementary table), and (b) the paper's own derived output — orthographically re-projected, geometrically controlled radiance maps plus cyclone velocity-vector fields — was released as ~150 MB of Nature Astronomy Source Data tables (SD1–SD3), not as a PDS bundle.
- Observation period used: JIRAM polar observations "between February 2017 and February 2019" (WebSearch synthesis of the paper; consistent with, not independently re-verified line-by-line against, the fetched full text).
