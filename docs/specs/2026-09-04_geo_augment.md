## Why
Archive labels carry no geometry for orbits >= 39 and for most 256-line
dual-band products; 64,139 of 85,108 frames have no label centre. The
catalog therefore needs SPICE-derived geometry for every frame and band
half. This task produces that table and settles one open fact: the
order of the L and M halves inside 256-line products.

## Facts
- Detector (IK `juno_jiram_v02.ti`, FK `juno_v12.tf`): the full imager
  frame `JUNO_JIRAM_I` has 266 lines; the L-band frame boresight is at
  +69 px along +X of the imager frame and the M-band boresight at -69 px
  (138 px apart, i.e. 128 + 10 gap lines). Each band frame is 128 x 432
  with IFOV 0.000237767 rad. Line 1 of a band is on its +X side.
- 256-line products (index `band == "LM"`, mode `SCI_I1_*`, 28,457
  frames, 442,368 bytes, float32 big-endian, `Last Index Fastest`) hold
  both bands with the 10 gap lines removed. Which half comes first is
  NOT documented (SIS v7.5 checked). The label's per-band groups give
  `LINE_FIRST_PIXEL = 2` for both halves, i.e. relative to each half.
- Orbit 24 has 944 frames, 98% `LM`, with label geometry for the M half
  on many of them and about 18% frames with `target_pixels == 0`.
  Orbit-4 image data is mirrored; orbit-24 image data is not (about
  420 MB; `jiram-catalog mirror --orbits 24 --kinds data --verify`).
- Image radiance unit W/(m^2 sr um); off-planet (deep space) pixels are
  near zero or negative after background subtraction; on-planet M-band
  pixels are strongly positive (thermal emission, day and night);
  on-planet L-band pixels are positive on the dayside (reflected
  sunlight) and near zero on the nightside except aurora. Use the M
  half's signal as the primary discriminator.

## Part A: `"LM"` support in `geometry.py`
`frame_geometry(epoch, "LM", kernels)` returns a `FrameGeometry` with
`lines == 256` whose per-pixel arrays are the vertical concatenation of
the L-band result (128 lines) and the M-band result (128 lines) in the
order given by the module constant `LM_HALF_ORDER: tuple[str, str]`
(default `("L", "M")` until Part C sets it; the constant must carry a
comment citing `docs/reports/lm_half_order.md`). `band` is `"LM"`,
`frame` is `"JUNO_JIRAM_I"`, `boresight` is the imager-frame boresight
computed the same way as for a band (sincpt on `JUNO_JIRAM_I` +Z),
`corners` are the four corners of the stacked 256-line array,
`ifov_rad` from `INS-61410_IFOV`. Two extra fields are added to
`FrameGeometry` with defaults so existing callers are unaffected:
`halves: tuple[str, ...] = ()` (e.g. `("L","M")`) and
`half_rows: tuple[tuple[int, int], ...] = ()` (0-based [start, stop)
row ranges of each half).

## Part B: `geo.py` and `jiram-catalog geo`
`jiram-catalog geo --orbits SPEC [--mirror PATH] [--jobs N] [--limit K]`
builds `<mirror>/index/frames_geo.parquet`, one row per (frame, band
half): a frame with `band` in {`L`,`M`} yields one row; `LM` yields two
rows (`half = "L"` and `half = "M"`), computed with `frame_geometry`
for the band frames at the frame's `start_time` (never the `LM`
stacked result; halves are geometrically independent). Rows for orbits
not selected are kept from an existing file; selected orbits are
replaced. `--limit K` processes only the first K frames per orbit
(testing aid). Parallelism: `multiprocessing` pool of `--jobs`
processes (default min(16, cpu count)), each worker loads
`KernelSet.for_orbits(mirror, [orbit])` once per orbit (SPICE state is
per process; never share kernels across processes) and processes that
orbit's frames; a frame whose computation raises records
`geo_ok=False` and the exception text, never aborting the run.

Columns (float64 unless noted; NaN when not on-planet):
`product_id:str`, `orbit_dir:int16`, `half:str` (`L` or `M`),
`start_time:datetime64[ns]`, `geo_ok:bool`, `geo_error:str`,
`on_planet_frac` (fraction of the 128x432 pixels on the ellipsoid),
`n_on_planet:int32`, `bore_lat`, `bore_lon_east`, `bore_emission`,
`bore_incidence`, `bore_phase`, `bore_range_km` (boresight sincpt;
NaN if off-planet), `c1_lat`,`c1_lon`,`c2_lat`,`c2_lon`,`c3_lat`,`c3_lon`,
`c4_lat`,`c4_lon` (corners as in `FrameGeometry.corners`),
`min_lat`, `max_lat` (over on-planet pixels), `lon_min_east`,
`lon_max_east` and `lon_span_deg` (smallest longitude arc containing all
on-planet pixels: compute by unwrapping longitudes relative to the
circular mean, then `lon_min_east`/`lon_max_east` are the arc's ends in
0-360 and `lon_span_deg` its length; if the arc exceeds 350 deg or the
frame contains a pole, set `pole_inside=True`, `lon_min_east=0`,
`lon_max_east=360`, `lon_span_deg=360`), `pole_inside:bool` (True when
the ellipsoid pole point lies inside the frame's on-planet footprint;
test: `max_lat > 89.5` for the north or `min_lat < -89.5` for the
south), `mean_emission`, `mean_incidence`, `min_emission`,
`max_emission` (over on-planet pixels), `median_pixel_km` (median over
on-planet pixels of `range_km * ifov_rad`), `sub_sc_lat`,
`sub_sc_lon_east`, `sc_altitude_km`, `local_time_h` (boresight local
solar time in hours: `(bore_lon_east - subsolar_lon_east)/15 + 12`
modulo 24 where subsolar longitude comes from
`subslr("NEAR POINT/ELLIPSOID","JUPITER",et,"IAU_JUPITER",abcorr,"JUNO")`;
NaN if the boresight is off-planet), `dayside_frac` (fraction of
on-planet pixels with incidence < 90 deg).
Parquet sorted by (`orbit_dir`, `start_time`, `product_id`, `half`).
Also provide `jiram_catalog.geo.load_geo(mirror, orbits=None)` and
`jiram_catalog.geo.frames_with_geo(mirror, orbits=None)` which merges
`load_frames` with `load_geo` on (`product_id`) and returns one row per
(frame, half) with all columns of both (index columns first, then geo
columns prefixed as they are, no renaming).

Consistency report printed at the end of `geo` (and written to
`<mirror>/index/geo_report.md`): rows, `geo_ok=False` count, and, for
rows where the label has a centre latitude for that half
(`frames.geom_band == half` and `center_lat` not null), the median and
95th-percentile great-circle distance between (`bore_lat`,
`bore_lon_east`) and (`center_lat`, `center_lon`), and the same for
`|bore_emission - emission_angle|` and `|min_lat - min_lat(label)|`.

## Part C: `scripts/lm_half_order.py` (run it; write the report)
1. `jiram-catalog mirror --orbits 24 --kinds data --jobs 2 --verify`.
2. Select orbit-24 `LM` frames with `0.05 <= on_planet_frac <= 0.95`
   for at least one half according to `frames_geo.parquet` (run `geo
   --orbits 24` first) and whose two halves have on-planet fractions
   differing by at least 0.2; take up to 40 such frames spread over the
   orbit (every k-th by time).
3. For each frame read the 256x432 float32 big-endian image, split into
   top (rows 0-127) and bottom (rows 128-255). For each half compute the
   fraction of pixels with radiance above a threshold `T` where `T` =
   5 x the median absolute deviation of the whole image above its
   median (document the exact formula in the report), giving
   `bright_top`, `bright_bottom`. Compare with the engine's on-planet
   fractions for the L and M band frames, `frac_L`, `frac_M`. Score
   hypothesis H1 (top = L, bottom = M) by
   `|bright_top - frac_L| + |bright_bottom - frac_M|` and H2 (top = M,
   bottom = L) by the swapped expression; the frame votes for the
   hypothesis with the smaller score, abstaining when the scores differ
   by less than 0.05.
4. Additionally, for frames where the M half is fully on-planet per the
   engine and the L half is not (or vice versa), report the mean
   radiance of top and bottom halves: the M-band on-planet mean must be
   clearly positive.
5. Write `docs/reports/lm_half_order.md` with a table (product_id,
   start_time, frac_L, frac_M, bright_top, bright_bottom, vote), the
   vote totals, and the conclusion. Set `LM_HALF_ORDER` in
   `geometry.py` accordingly only if at least 80% of non-abstaining
   votes agree and there are at least 10 votes; otherwise leave the
   default and state in the report that the order is unresolved.
